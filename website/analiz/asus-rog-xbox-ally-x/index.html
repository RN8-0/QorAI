Detailed analysis

The Asus ROG Xbox Ally X (AMD Ryzen AI Z2 Extreme) is a high-performance portable gaming console designed for enthusiasts seeking a powerful handheld experience. It features the advanced AMD Ryzen AI Z2 Extreme processor, offering substantial performance gains over its predecessors, particularly in AAA gaming titles. The console is equipped with 24 GB of fast LPDDR5X-8000 RAM and a 1 TB PCIe 4.0 NVMe M.2 SSD, ensuring quick load times and smooth multitasking. Performance is a key strength, with the AMD Ryzen AI Z2 Extreme delivering up to a 30% boost in demanding games. The integrated AMD RDNA 3.5 graphics processor, with 16 CUs and a boost clock of 2.7 GHz, provides robust visual fidelity. The cooling system is efficient, maintaining GPU temperatures around 57°C even under heavy load, though fan noise can be noticeable in 'Turbo' mode. Compatibility is excellent due to its Windows 11 Home operating system, which opens up the entire PC gaming ecosystem. Users can access platforms like Steam, Epic Games, and Xbox Game Pass without limitations. The console also supports modern connectivity standards including Wi-Fi 6E and Bluetooth 5.4, ensuring compatibility with a wide range of peripherals and networks. Longevity is supported by the upgradeable 1 TB PCIe 4.0 NVMe M.2 SSD, which uses the M.2 2280 form factor for easy user replacement or expansion. The inclusion of a USB4/Thunderbolt 4 port also future-proofs the device, allowing for high-speed external peripherals and potentially eGPU solutions. The 80 Whr battery is a significant upgrade, promising extended gaming sessions. Potential risks include initial software glitches and the 'fiddly' nature of the Xbox Full Screen Experience, which may require updates to fully mature. While the 7-inch 120Hz IPS display is high-quality, some users might find it small compared to competitors with larger screens or OLED panels, impacting immersion for certain titles. Buying advice for this console is generally positive for serious portable gamers. Its combination of raw power, extended battery life, and open Windows ecosystem makes it a versatile choice. Users prioritizing the absolute best in handheld performance and a vast game library will find this device highly compelling, despite its premium price point. Consider this device if you are looking for a handheld that can run demanding PC games with high frame rates. The ergonomic design and robust build quality also contribute to a premium feel. However, be prepared for potential software refinements and the learning curve associated with Windows on a handheld form factor.

Things that change the decision

Display Technology and Size

The 7-inch IPS display, while offering 120Hz and FHD resolution, may feel small to some users compared to rivals with larger screens. Its IPS panel also means it might fall behind OLED competitors in contrast and brightness.

Storage Upgradeability

The M.2 2280 form factor for SSD upgrades is convenient, but users must be comfortable with opening the device to perform this upgrade. This might be a barrier for less technically inclined individuals.

USB4/Thunderbolt 4 Support

The inclusion of a USB4/Thunderbolt 4 port is excellent for future-proofing and external GPU support. However, realizing its full potential often requires purchasing additional, often expensive, accessories.

What owners say

Satisfaction: 80/100

Community sentiment for the Asus ROG Xbox Ally X is largely positive, with a strong emphasis on its raw gaming performance. Many owners are impressed by the AMD Ryzen AI Z2 Extreme processor, noting its capability to handle modern AAA titles with respectable frame rates. The significant upgrade in battery life to 80 Whr has also been widely celebrated, addressing a common concern with previous portable gaming devices and enabling longer play sessions. The ergonomic design, featuring comfortable grips inspired by Xbox controllers, receives consistent praise for enhancing the user experience during extended gameplay. This attention to physical comfort contributes significantly to overall satisfaction, making it a preferred choice for long gaming marathons. The upgradeable 1 TB NVMe SSD is another frequently lauded feature, offering users the flexibility to expand their game library without hassle. However, the software experience elicits more mixed reactions. While Windows 11 provides an open ecosystem for games, the Xbox Full Screen Experience (FSE) is often described as 'fiddly' or requiring occasional exits for certain functions, which can disrupt the seamless gaming flow. Initial software glitches and delayed feature updates also contributed to some early user frustration, though ongoing updates are expected to improve this over time. The 7-inch IPS display, despite its 120Hz refresh rate and FHD resolution, draws some criticism regarding its size. A segment of the community feels it is too small compared to some competitors, impacting immersion. While the display quality is generally good, the lack of an OLED panel means it doesn't offer the same vibrant contrast and deep blacks as some rivals, leading to a slightly negative perception in this area. Overall, the Asus ROG Xbox Ally X is seen as a powerful and capable handheld, particularly for those prioritizing performance and an open game library. The positive feedback on hardware performance, battery life, and upgradeability outweighs the concerns about software polish and screen size for many users, indicating a generally satisfied owner base.

Smart alternatives

  • MSI Claw A8 — The MSI Claw A8 offers similar CPU core count and an identical 80 Whr battery capacity, making it a direct competitor in terms of portable power and endurance. It uses a WUXGA (1920x1200p) resolution display, slightly different from the Ally X's FHD, potentially offering a slightly taller aspect ratio for some games. Both devices aim for high-performance handheld gaming.
  • Steam Deck OLED (1 TB) — The Steam Deck OLED (1 TB) provides a significantly different experience with its OLED display, offering superior contrast and vibrant colors compared to the Ally X's IPS panel. However, it features a less powerful 4-core CPU and lower graphics processing power (1.6 TFLOPs vs 5.53 TFLOPs), along with a smaller 50 Whr battery. It runs SteamOS, a Linux-based operating system, which provides a more console-like experience but has a more limited native game library than Windows.
  • Asus ROG Xbox Ally (AMD Ryzen Z2 A) — This is the direct predecessor, offering a similar form factor but with a less powerful AMD Ryzen Z2 A processor (4 cores) and lower graphics processing power (1.63 TFLOPs). It also has a smaller 60 Whr battery and 16 GB of RAM, compared to the Ally X's 80 Whr battery and 24 GB RAM. The original Ally is a more budget-friendly option, but with clear compromises in performance and endurance.
