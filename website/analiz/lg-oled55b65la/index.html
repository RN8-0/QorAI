Detailed analysis

The LG OLED55B65LA Ultra HD (4K) TV, released in 2026, represents a high-end offering in the television market, leveraging LG's renowned OLED technology. Its 55-inch OLED panel is the cornerstone of its visual prowess, delivering self-emissive pixels that produce perfect blacks and infinite contrast, which are critical for an immersive viewing experience. The α8 (Alpha8) (Gen3) AI Processor 4K intelligently optimizes image quality, performing 4K upscaling and dynamic tone mapping to enhance brightness and detail across all content. Performance and quality are exceptional, particularly in picture fidelity. The TV supports a true 100/120/144 Hz refresh rate, with HDMI 2.1 inputs capable of handling 4K signals at up to 144 Hz. This, combined with a rapid 0.1 ms response time, makes it a standout choice for fast-action sports and competitive gaming. HDR support is comprehensive, including Dolby Vision, HDR10, and HLG, ensuring vibrant and accurate color reproduction with the DCI-P3 100% color gamut. Compatibility is broad, with four HDMI 2.1 ports offering advanced features like eARC, QMS, and QFT, making it future-proof for various external devices, including next-generation gaming consoles and sound systems. Wireless connectivity is robust with Wi-Fi 5 and Bluetooth 5.3, alongside AirPlay 2 and Apple HomeKit support for seamless integration into smart home ecosystems. The webOS 26 smart platform is intuitive and responsive, providing access to a wide range of streaming services and AI-driven features. Longevity is a key consideration for OLED technology. While OLED panels offer superior picture quality, they carry a risk of burn-in from static images over prolonged periods. LG has implemented protective measures like pixel shifting and pixel cleaning to mitigate this, but it remains a factor for users who frequently display static content. The TV's build quality and software support, including four software updates within five years, suggest a reasonable lifespan for the device. Potential risks primarily revolve around the inherent characteristics of OLED technology, specifically the burn-in potential and occasional panel uniformity issues. While these are generally rare with modern OLEDs and proper usage, they are points for buyers to be aware of. The energy consumption, particularly in HDR mode, is also higher than some LED alternatives, which could impact long-term operating costs. Buying advice for the LG OLED55B65LA leans heavily towards enthusiasts of high-quality visual content and serious gamers. Its combination of an advanced OLED panel, powerful AI processing, and extensive gaming features positions it as a premium choice. For those seeking the absolute best in picture quality and a highly responsive gaming experience, this model delivers significantly. However, buyers should factor in the potential need for an external audio solution and be mindful of OLED-specific considerations.

Things that change the decision

OLED Burn-in Risk

OLED panels can be susceptible to burn-in from static images over extended periods, though modern TVs include mitigation features. LG's standard warranty typically does not cover burn-in, which is a significant consideration for prolonged static content viewing.

Energy Consumption

As an OLED TV with advanced features, its energy consumption, particularly in HDR mode (122 kWh/1000h, Class G), is relatively high. This could lead to higher electricity bills compared to more energy-efficient LED models.
