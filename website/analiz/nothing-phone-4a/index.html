Detailed analysis

The Nothing Phone (4a) enters the smartphone market as a distinctive upper-mid-range device, building on Nothing's reputation for unique design and a clean software experience. Powered by the Qualcomm Snapdragon 7s Gen 4 chipset, it aims to deliver a balanced performance for everyday users. Its transparent aesthetic and Glyph Bar are key differentiators, offering a visual appeal that stands out from competitors. Performance-wise, the Snapdragon 7s Gen 4, with its 8-core configuration, provides a smooth and responsive experience for general usage and moderate gaming. The lightweight Nothing OS significantly optimizes hardware utilization, leading to faster app launches and efficient multitasking with 8 GB of RAM, expandable up to 20 GB. The upgrade to UFS 3.1 storage also ensures quicker data access compared to its predecessor. The camera system is a strong suit, featuring a 50 MP main camera with OIS and EIS, alongside a 50 MP telephoto lens offering 3.5x optical zoom. These cameras perform admirably in well-lit conditions, capturing detailed photos and 4K videos. The 32 MP front camera is also capable for selfies and video calls. However, the ultra-wide camera's performance is less consistent, and video stabilization can show minor jitters when moving. The 6.78-inch AMOLED display is a highlight, boasting a 120Hz refresh rate, 1224 x 2720 pixel resolution, and impressive peak brightness of 4500 nits. Protected by Corning Gorilla Glass 7i, it offers vivid colors and excellent fluidity. This makes content consumption and general interaction highly enjoyable, despite some user comments about slightly thicker bezels. Battery life from the 5080 mAh unit is respectable, providing approximately 13.5 hours of mixed usage. The 50W fast charging is a welcome feature, allowing the device to reach a full charge in about 64 minutes. This ensures minimal downtime for users. However, some reports of quicker battery drain under minimal use suggest potential software optimization needs. Nothing's commitment to software support is commendable, offering three major Android updates and six years of security patches for Nothing OS 4.1, based on Android 16. This provides excellent longevity and ensures the device remains current with the latest features and security enhancements. The OS itself is praised for its simplicity and unique visual elements like Live Updates on the Glyph Bar. In terms of build, the Nothing Phone (4a) features an IP64 rating for dust and splash resistance, along with a sturdy polycarbonate frame. The transparent design and Glyph Bar are central to its identity, providing both aesthetic appeal and functional notifications. While the haptic feedback is good, it might not feel as premium as some higher-end devices. Connectivity options are comprehensive, including dual SIM, Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.4, and NFC. The inclusion of a USB Type-C port for charging and audio output is standard. The box contents are practical, including a SIM ejector, USB-C cable, and a pre-applied screen protector. However, the notable absence of wireless charging is a significant drawback for a device in this price segment.

  • Haptic feedback, while good, may not feel as premium as higher-end models.

Things that change the decision

Lack of Wireless Charging

The Nothing Phone (4a) does not support wireless charging, which is a significant omission for a device in its segment. This might be a deal-breaker for users accustomed to the convenience of cable-free charging.

Performance Limitations

While adequate for daily use, the Snapdragon 7s Gen 4 chipset may not meet the demands of intensive gaming or heavy multitasking. Users requiring peak performance for demanding applications should consider alternatives.

Heating Reports

Some owners have reported noticeable heating during sustained use, which could impact comfort and long-term component health. While not universal, this recurring feedback suggests a potential thermal management concern.

Battery Drain Concerns

Despite a sizable battery, a subset of users experienced faster-than-expected battery drain, even with minimal usage. This could lead to more frequent charging for some users than the average battery life suggests.
