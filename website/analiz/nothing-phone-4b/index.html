Detailed analysis

The Nothing Phone (4b) enters the smartphone market as a mid-range contender, aiming to deliver a balanced experience with its distinctive design and software philosophy. It is powered by the Qualcomm Snapdragon 6 Gen 4 chipset, fabricated on a 4nm process, which provides a solid foundation for daily tasks and moderate gaming. The device's commitment to a clean Android experience through Nothing OS is a significant draw for users seeking simplicity and efficiency. Performance-wise, the Snapdragon 6 Gen 4, with its 8-core CPU configuration, offers a noticeable improvement over previous generations, as claimed by Nothing. While it handles routine applications and multitasking smoothly, it may not compete with higher-tier chipsets found in more expensive rivals, especially under sustained heavy loads. The integrated cooling system, featuring a large vapor chamber, aims to maintain thermal stability during intensive use. The camera system comprises a 50 MP main sensor with OIS and an 8 MP ultra-wide lens, delivering competent photography in favorable lighting conditions. Photos are characterized by natural colors and adequate detail, making it suitable for casual photography. However, the absence of a telephoto lens limits zoom capabilities, and some users might find the default image processing to be overly contrasty, affecting dynamic range in certain scenarios. Its 6.77-inch Super AMOLED display is a highlight, offering a sharp FHD+ resolution and a fluid 120 Hz adaptive refresh rate. With HDR10+ support and high peak brightness, the screen provides an immersive and vibrant viewing experience, excelling in media consumption. The inclusion of Dragontrail Pro Glass ensures reasonable durability against scratches and minor impacts. Battery life is a strong suit, with the global version featuring a 5200 mAh cell that easily lasts a full day for most users. While 33W fast charging is available, allowing for a relatively quick recharge, the lack of wireless charging is a notable omission that might deter some buyers. Regional variations in battery capacity, with some markets receiving a larger 6000 mAh battery, should be considered. Nothing's software commitment is commendable, promising three major Android updates and six years of security patches. Nothing OS 4.1, based on Android 16, offers a bloatware-free, responsive, and aesthetically pleasing interface, incorporating unique features like the Glyph interface and essential AI tools. This long-term support enhances the device's longevity and value. Build quality features a polycarbonate unibody and an IP64 rating, providing decent protection against dust and splashes. The semi-transparent design and refined Glyph interface contribute to its distinctive aesthetic. While not ruggedized, its construction feels solid for daily handling and minor environmental exposures. Connectivity options are comprehensive, including 5G, Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 6.0, and dual SIM support. However, NFC availability varies by region, and the device's optimization for US networks is limited, potentially affecting wireless performance for users in that market. These regional discrepancies are important for buyers to verify. Overall, the Nothing Phone (4b) presents a compelling package for its price segment, particularly for users who prioritize a high-quality display, extended battery life, and a clean, well-supported software experience. Its trade-offs, such as moderate performance for demanding tasks and the absence of wireless charging, are typical for its category but should be weighed against its strengths. The regional differences in features like battery capacity and NFC support necessitate careful consideration based on the intended market.

Things that change the decision

The battery capacity varies by region, with some markets receiving a larger 6000 mAh battery while the global version has 5200 mAh. This difference impacts overall battery life expectations depending on purchase location.

Limited US Network Optimization

The device is not optimized for US networks, which could result in slower wireless speeds or inconsistent connectivity for users in that region. This is a significant consideration for potential buyers in the US.

NFC Support Varies by Region

NFC functionality, essential for contactless payments and quick pairing, is not available in all markets. Buyers should verify NFC availability in their specific region before purchase.

What owners say

Satisfaction: 75/100

Community sentiment for the Nothing Phone (4b) is generally positive, with a significant portion of owners expressing satisfaction with their purchase. The device's Super AMOLED display is a standout feature, consistently lauded for its brightness, color accuracy, and smooth 120 Hz refresh rate, which greatly enhances the overall viewing experience for media consumption and general navigation. Another highly appreciated aspect is the battery life, with many users confirming that the 5200 mAh (or 6000 mAh in some regions) battery easily provides a full day of use, often extending into a second day with moderate usage. The clean and intuitive Nothing OS also garners strong praise; users value its minimalist design, lack of bloatware, and the unique Glyph interface, contributing to a fluid and enjoyable software experience. However, the sentiment is not entirely uniform. While performance is generally considered adequate for everyday tasks, some users note that it doesn't quite match the raw power of competitors in the same price bracket, particularly for heavy gaming or intensive multitasking. This leads to a neutral stance on performance, acknowledging its sufficiency but also its limitations. The most frequently cited negative point revolves around the absence of wireless charging. Many owners express disappointment, as this feature is becoming standard in the mid-range segment and is seen as a significant convenience. Additionally, some feedback indicates that the camera's default image processing can be overly contrasty, leading to less natural-looking photos in certain lighting conditions, though the overall camera reliability is not widely questioned. Overall, the Nothing Phone (4b) is well-received for its core strengths in display, battery, and software. The areas of concern, such as wireless charging and camera processing, are acknowledged but do not overshadow the positive aspects for the majority of users, who appreciate its distinctive design and user-centric approach.
