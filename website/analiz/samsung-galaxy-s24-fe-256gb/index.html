Detailed analysis

The Samsung Galaxy S24 FE (256 GB) emerges as a compelling smartphone in the mid-to-high segment, blending premium features with a more accessible price point. Its core strength lies in its robust hardware, spearheaded by the Samsung Exynos 2400e chipset, which ensures capable performance for a wide range of tasks from daily browsing to more intensive gaming. Performance-wise, the device, equipped with 8 GB of RAM, handles multitasking and demanding applications with notable fluidity. While some users have reported minor fluctuations in battery performance following software updates, the overall speed and responsiveness remain a significant advantage, contributing to a smooth user experience. The camera system is a standout feature, boasting a versatile 50 MP main sensor, an ultra-wide lens, and a telephoto camera with 3x optical zoom. This setup consistently delivers excellent image quality, characterized by accurate exposure, vibrant colors, and reliable autofocus, rivaling even some flagship models in its photographic capabilities. Its 6.7-inch Dynamic AMOLED 2X display is another highlight, offering a stunning visual experience with its FHD+ resolution, 120 Hz refresh rate, and impressive 1900 nits peak brightness. Protected by Corning Gorilla Glass Victus+, the screen is both beautiful and durable, although some users have noted occasional touch screen responsiveness issues. Battery life, while generally sufficient for a full day of moderate use with its 4700 mAh capacity, has been a point of contention for some users who expected more. The 25W fast charging and wireless charging capabilities are welcome, but the absence of a charging adapter in the box is a minor inconvenience that adds to the initial cost. Software support is a major draw, with Samsung committing to 7 years of updates, including Android OS upgrades and security patches. This extended support, combined with the intuitive One UI 6.1.1 interface and integrated Galaxy AI features, ensures the device remains current and functional for an extended period. In terms of build, the aluminum frame and glass construction, coupled with an IP68 rating for water and dust resistance, underscore its premium feel and durability. However, isolated reports of minor manufacturing quirks suggest some variability in quality control, which potential buyers should be aware of. Overall, the Samsung Galaxy S24 FE (256 GB) is a strong contender for users prioritizing camera performance, display quality, and long-term software support. Its trade-offs, primarily in battery consistency and the occasional touch screen issue, are minor considerations against its overall value proposition. It represents a well-rounded package for those seeking a near-premium experience without the flagship price tag.

  • Seven years of promised software updates ensure long-term usability and access to the latest Android features and security patches.

Weaknesses

  • Battery life, while generally adequate, can be inconsistent for heavy users, with some reporting reduced screen-on time after updates.
  • The 25W fast charging speed is not class-leading, and the absence of a charging adapter in the box adds to the total cost.
  • Some users have reported occasional touch screen responsiveness issues, which could impact the overall user experience.
  • The Exynos 2400e chipset, while powerful, has been linked to some battery optimization concerns by a segment of users.
  • While durable, isolated reports of minor manufacturing quirks suggest potential inconsistencies in build quality for some units.

What owners say

Satisfaction: 75/100

Community sentiment for the Samsung Galaxy S24 FE (256 GB) is largely positive, with a significant majority of owners expressing satisfaction with their purchase. The device is particularly lauded for its exceptional camera capabilities, which many users find comparable to or even surpassing those of higher-end flagships. The vibrant and fluid Dynamic AMOLED 2X display also garners widespread acclaim for its visual quality and smooth operation. Another strong point frequently highlighted by the community is Samsung's impressive commitment to 7 years of software updates. This long-term support is seen as a major advantage, ensuring the device remains current and secure for an extended period, which resonates well with users looking for longevity in their smartphone investment. However, the positive sentiment is tempered by some recurring concerns. Battery life emerges as a notable point of contention, with a segment of users reporting that the 4700 mAh battery, while decent on paper, doesn't always translate to consistent all-day performance, especially after certain software updates. This inconsistency can be frustrating for heavy users. Another area of concern, though less widespread, revolves around reported touch screen issues. Some owners have experienced problems such as unresponsiveness during typing or phantom touches, which can detract from the overall user experience. While not a universal complaint, it's a significant enough issue to be a recurring theme in discussions. Performance, driven by the Exynos 2400e processor, generally receives positive feedback for its ability to handle daily tasks and even demanding games smoothly. However, the Exynos chipset has also been indirectly linked to some of the battery optimization concerns, creating a split in opinion regarding its overall efficiency.
