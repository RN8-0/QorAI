Detailed analysis

The Samsung 75M80H Ultra HD (4K) TV, released in 2026, is a 75-inch Mini LED Smart TV running on Samsung's Tizen operating system. It features a 4K resolution panel, aiming to deliver a premium viewing experience with advanced image processing and smart capabilities. The TV is designed to cater to users seeking a large, high-resolution display with modern connectivity and smart features. At its core, the 75M80H leverages Mini LED technology and the NQ4 AI Gen2 Processor 4K to enhance brightness, contrast, and overall clarity. The AI-powered image processing is particularly effective at upscaling lower-resolution content to near-4K quality, making older media look better on the large screen. However, the lack of a true local dimming feature means that while overall brightness is good, black levels in dark scenes may appear less deep and more washed out compared to TVs with more advanced dimming zones. Performance-wise, the 144Hz Mini LED panel is a standout feature, providing exceptionally fluid motion crucial for gaming and fast-action sports. For PC gamers, it even supports up to 240Hz DLG (Dynamic Refresh Rate) at Full HD. This high refresh rate, combined with gaming-specific features like FreeSync Premium, VRR, and ALLM, ensures a highly responsive and tear-free gaming experience. The TV also supports HDR10+ and HLG, with Mini LED HDR and Auto HDR Remastering attempting to improve dynamic range, though its peak HDR brightness is not class-leading. Connectivity is robust, featuring three HDMI 2.1 inputs, which are essential for next-generation consoles and high-bandwidth devices, alongside a USB-A port, Wi-Fi 5, and Bluetooth 5.3. The inclusion of HDMI eARC simplifies connecting soundbars and AV receivers. However, the absence of a dedicated digital audio output might be a minor inconvenience for some users with specific legacy audio setups. The Tizen smart platform is a mature and user-friendly system, offering a wide array of streaming applications and smart home integration through Bixby, Amazon Alexa, and Google Assistant compatibility. This ensures a seamless and intuitive user experience for navigating content and controlling smart devices. The TV's audio system, with 20W RMS and 2.0 speakers, is enhanced by Adaptive Sound Plus and Object Tracking Sound (OTS Lite), providing clear dialogue and a balanced sound profile, though it may benefit from an external sound system for a truly immersive experience. Longevity is supported by Samsung's commitment to 7 years of software updates, which is a significant advantage for a Smart TV. This ensures that the Tizen platform and its applications remain current and secure for an extended period. The build quality is generally solid, consistent with Samsung's reputation, though potential panel uniformity issues have been noted by some owners, which could impact long-term visual consistency. Potential risks include the aforementioned lack of local dimming, which can be a significant drawback for cinephiles prioritizing deep blacks and high contrast in dark viewing environments. The HDR brightness, while good, may not satisfy those accustomed to the very brightest HDR displays. Buyers should also be aware of the reported occasional panel uniformity issues, which, while not widespread, could affect the viewing experience. Overall, the Samsung 75M80H is a strong choice for gamers and those who value a large, high-refresh-rate screen with excellent smart features. Its AI-powered upscaling and vibrant colours make it suitable for general entertainment, especially in brighter rooms. However, buyers seeking the absolute best in contrast and HDR performance for dedicated home cinema setups might need to consider alternatives with more advanced local dimming solutions.

Strengths

  • Features a large 75-inch Mini LED panel for an immersive viewing experience.
  • Offers a high 144Hz refresh rate, ideal for smooth gaming and sports content.
  • Equipped with three HDMI 2.1 inputs, supporting next-gen gaming features like VRR and ALLM.
  • Includes advanced AI-powered 4K upscaling for enhancing lower-resolution content.
  • Runs on the Tizen operating system, providing a user-friendly and feature-rich smart TV experience.
  • Supports FreeSync Premium and VRR, ensuring tear-free and low-latency gaming.

Weaknesses

  • Lacks a true local dimming feature, which can lead to less precise black levels and contrast.
  • HDR peak brightness is not as high as some competitors, potentially limiting the impact of HDR content.
  • Reported slower pixel response times may cause some motion blur in fast-moving scenes.
  • The TV does not include a dedicated digital audio output, requiring HDMI eARC for external audio.
  • The 20W RMS audio output, while adequate, may not be powerful enough for a truly immersive sound experience on a 75-inch screen.

Things that change the decision

HDR Brightness Limitations

While supporting HDR10+ and HLG, the TV's peak HDR brightness is not as high as some competing models. This can result in a less dynamic and impactful HDR experience, particularly in very bright or very dark scenes where highlights might not pop as much.

What owners say

Satisfaction: 78/100

Community sentiment for the Samsung 75M80H Ultra HD (4K) TV is generally positive, with a notable emphasis on its strengths in gaming and overall visual enhancement. Many owners are particularly impressed with the AI-driven image upscaling, which breathes new life into older content by bringing it closer to 4K quality. The large 75-inch screen size is also a frequently cited positive, contributing to a highly immersive viewing experience that users find compelling for movies and sports. Gamers, in particular, form a strong positive segment, consistently praising the TV's 144Hz refresh rate, low input lag, and support for features like FreeSync Premium and VRR. These capabilities are seen as crucial for a fluid and responsive gaming experience, making the TV a popular choice among this demographic. The Tizen smart platform also receives positive feedback for its intuitive interface and broad app support, contributing to overall user satisfaction. However, a significant point of contention revolves around the TV's picture quality in specific scenarios. The lack of a robust local dimming feature is a recurring complaint, with users noting that black levels in dark scenes can appear washed out or less deep than expected. This limitation particularly impacts the cinematic experience for those who frequently watch content in dimly lit environments, leading to some negative feedback. Another area of mixed sentiment concerns HDR performance. While the TV supports HDR10+ and HLG, some owners feel that the peak HDR brightness is not as impactful as on other premium TVs, leading to a less dynamic HDR presentation. This suggests that while the TV offers HDR compatibility, it may not fully deliver the 'pop' that some users expect from a modern 4K HDR display. Audio quality from the built-in speakers is generally considered adequate for everyday viewing but rarely stands out as a strong point. Many users find the 20W RMS output sufficient for casual use, but those seeking a more immersive sound experience often plan for or invest in external sound systems. This indicates a neutral stance on the integrated audio, where it meets basic expectations but doesn't exceed them.
