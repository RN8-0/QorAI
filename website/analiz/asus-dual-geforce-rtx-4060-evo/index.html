Detailed analysis

The Asus Dual GeForce RTX 4060 EVO screen card is built on NVIDIA's Ada Lovelace architecture, specifically the GeForce RTX 4060 GPU, fabricated on a 5 nm process. It features 3072 shading units and 24 ray tracing cores, delivering a graphics processing power of 15.1 TFLOPS (FP32). With 8GB of GDDR6 memory across a 128-bit interface, it achieves a memory bandwidth of 272 GB/s and an effective memory speed of 17 Gbps. The card supports PCIe 4.0 and DirectX 12 Ultimate, making it compatible with modern systems and APIs. Performance-wise, this card excels in 1080p gaming, consistently delivering high frame rates even in demanding titles. It leverages NVIDIA's DLSS 3 for AI-accelerated upscaling and frame generation, significantly boosting performance in supported games. While capable of some 1440p gaming, it often requires detail compromises or heavy reliance on DLSS. Its ray tracing capabilities are present but, like most cards in this tier, are best utilized with DLSS enabled for playable frame rates. Compatibility is a strong suit, thanks to its compact 227mm length and 2.5-slot design, making it suitable for a wide range of PC cases, including smaller form factor builds. The low 115W TDP means it can run efficiently with a modest 550W power supply, reducing the need for expensive PSU upgrades. It requires a single 8-pin power connector, which is standard and widely available. For longevity, the card supports current technologies like DLSS 3 and ray tracing, offering a degree of future-proofing for graphics features. However, the 8GB VRAM and 128-bit memory bus are potential limitations for sustained high-fidelity gaming at 1080p or higher resolutions over a 4+ year period, as newer games become more VRAM-intensive. Users prioritizing long-term ultra-settings performance might find this a bottleneck. The primary risk for buyers is the potential for coil whine, which some owners have reported, particularly at idle. While not affecting performance, it can be a nuisance for users who prioritize silence. The 8GB VRAM limitation is another consideration, as it might necessitate earlier upgrades or graphical compromises in future titles, impacting the perceived long-term value. Buying advice for this card is straightforward: it is an excellent choice for dedicated 1080p gamers who value consistent high frame rates, power efficiency, and quiet operation in a compact build. It offers a balanced approach to modern features like ray tracing and DLSS 3 without demanding a premium price. However, those aiming for 1440p gaming or expecting to keep the card for many years without significant graphical compromises should carefully consider the VRAM limitation. It represents a strong value proposition for its target segment. The Asus Dual GeForce RTX 4060 EVO is a well-engineered card that delivers on its promises for its intended audience. Its strengths in efficiency, size, and quiet performance make it a compelling option for specific build requirements and gaming priorities. The Dual BIOS feature adds practical utility, allowing users to easily switch between performance and quiet modes based on their immediate needs without software intervention.

Things that change the decision

VRAM Limitation for Longevity

The 8GB GDDR6 VRAM, coupled with a 128-bit memory bus, is a significant constraint for future-proofing, especially if you expect to play new, demanding titles at 1080p ultra settings or consider 1440p in 4+ years. This could force earlier upgrades or significant graphical compromises.

Coil Whine Potential

Some owners have reported coil whine, particularly at idle or during web browsing, which could be an annoyance if silence is paramount. While not performance-impacting, it detracts from the quiet operation promise.

ASUS Software Suite

While the card offers Dual BIOS for easy profile switching, some users find the broader ASUS software suite clunky. This could impact the user experience for fine-tuning or monitoring if you prefer integrated software control.

What owners say

Satisfaction: 75/100

The Asus Dual GeForce RTX 4060 EVO generally receives positive feedback, primarily from users focused on 1080p gaming. Many owners are highly satisfied with its performance in this resolution, noting its capability to run modern titles with good frame rates. The card's power efficiency and quiet operation, particularly due to its 0dB fan technology, are frequently lauded, making it a popular choice for those building compact or silent PCs. Its physical dimensions are also a significant advantage for users with limited case space. However, community sentiment is not uniformly positive, with a notable split concerning its long-term viability. A significant number of users express strong reservations about the 8GB VRAM, viewing it as a potential bottleneck for future games, especially if aiming for higher texture settings or resolutions beyond 1080p. This concern directly impacts the perception of its value for money and future-proofing capabilities, leading some to consider it a less compelling upgrade. Another point of contention, though less widespread than the VRAM discussion, is the presence of coil whine. While not all owners experience it, those who do find it to be a noticeable drawback, especially given the card's otherwise quiet design. This issue can detract from the overall user experience for sensitive individuals. The perceived modest generational performance uplift over the RTX 3060 also contributes to some neutral sentiment, as some users expected a more substantial raw power increase. Overall, the consensus leans towards recommending the Asus Dual GeForce RTX 4060 EVO for its specific niche: efficient, quiet 1080p gaming in a compact form factor. However, buyers are advised to be aware of the VRAM limitation and the possibility of coil whine, which are the main points of disagreement among owners and reviewers. The card's strong points are well-evidenced by multiple sources, while the drawbacks are also consistently highlighted.

Who is it for?

Skip it if: This card is not ideal for users primarily targeting 1440p or 4K gaming, those seeking maximum future-proofing for 4+ years without VRAM concerns, or individuals expecting a dramatic generational performance leap from an RTX 3060.
