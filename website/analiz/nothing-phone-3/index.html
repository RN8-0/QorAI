Detailed analysis

The Nothing Phone (3) enters the smartphone market with a distinctive design and a focus on a clean software experience. It is powered by the Qualcomm Snapdragon 8s Gen 4 chipset, fabricated on a 4 nm process, which promises significant performance gains over its predecessor. The device features a 6.67-inch FHD+ AMOLED display with a 120 Hz adaptive refresh rate, aiming to deliver a fluid visual experience. Performance-wise, the Snapdragon 8s Gen 4 ensures that the Nothing Phone (3) handles daily tasks and demanding applications with ease. Users can expect smooth app transitions and capable gaming performance, although some benchmarks suggest it doesn't quite reach the absolute peak of current flagship processors. The 12 GB of RAM further supports robust multitasking capabilities. The camera system comprises a versatile triple 50 MP setup, including a main sensor, a periscope telephoto with 3x optical zoom, and an ultrawide lens, complemented by a 50 MP front camera. While capable of capturing detailed images with good color accuracy, particularly in macro mode, the system has shown inconsistencies with focus and struggles in low-light environments, which might not satisfy photography enthusiasts. Compatibility is generally strong, with support for 5G, Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6.0, and NFC. It also features dual SIM and eSIM capabilities, offering flexibility for connectivity. However, the inclusion of a USB Type-C 2.0 port is a notable limitation, as it restricts data transfer speeds compared to the faster USB 3.1 standard commonly found in competing devices. Longevity is a key selling point, with Nothing committing to five years of Android updates and seven years of security patches. This extended software support ensures the device remains current and secure for a considerable period, enhancing its value over time. The build quality, featuring a metal frame and Gorilla Glass protection, also contributes to its expected durability. Potential risks include the camera's inconsistent performance, which might disappoint users expecting flagship-level photography. The USB Type-C 2.0 port is another area of concern for those who frequently transfer large files. Additionally, the device's weight and thickness might not appeal to users who prefer a more compact and lighter form factor. For buyers, the Nothing Phone (3) offers a unique blend of style, performance, and long-term software commitment. It's an excellent choice for users who appreciate a minimalist, fluid Android experience and a standout design. However, those prioritizing cutting-edge camera performance or rapid wired data transfer might find better options elsewhere. The device's IP68 rating and robust build add to its appeal for everyday use.

Strengths

  • Distinctive transparent design with Glyph Interface offers a unique aesthetic.
  • Powerful Qualcomm Snapdragon 8s Gen 4 processor ensures smooth and responsive performance.
  • Nothing OS 3.5 provides a clean, fast, and ad-free Android experience with unique widgets.
  • Extended software support with five years of Android updates and seven years of security patches.
  • Robust build quality featuring a metal frame and Gorilla Glass 7i/Victus protection.
  • IP68 water and dust resistance provides enhanced durability against environmental elements.

Weaknesses

  • USB Type-C 2.0 port limits data transfer speeds, falling behind modern standards.
  • Camera performance can be inconsistent, with focus issues and struggles in low-light conditions.
  • The device is heavier (218g) and thicker (9mm) than many direct competitors.
  • Lacks an LTPO display panel, which could offer better power efficiency.
  • Glass back is prone to fingerprints and smudges, requiring frequent cleaning.

Things that change the decision

Battery Capacity Differences

The Nothing Phone (3) has a 5150 mAh battery in international models, while some regions, like India, receive a larger 5500 mAh capacity. This regional variation means some buyers might get a slightly less enduring device.

What owners say

Satisfaction: 75/100

Community sentiment for the Nothing Phone (3) is generally positive, largely driven by its distinctive design and the highly praised Nothing OS experience. Many owners are enthusiastic about the unique Glyph Interface and the phone's overall aesthetic, finding it a refreshing departure from conventional smartphone designs. The performance, powered by the Snapdragon 8s Gen 4, also receives strong positive feedback, with users reporting a consistently fluid and responsive experience across various applications and games. However, opinions diverge significantly when it comes to the camera system. While some users find the image quality satisfactory for casual use, a substantial portion expresses disappointment with its inconsistencies, particularly regarding focus accuracy and low-light performance. This suggests that while the camera is capable, it doesn't consistently deliver the premium results expected at this price point, leading to a split among users who prioritize photography. Battery life and charging speed elicit a more neutral response. While the 65W fast charging is appreciated for quick top-ups, actual battery endurance varies among users, with some reporting excellent screen-on times and others finding it merely average. This suggests that software optimization or individual usage patterns play a significant role in perceived battery performance. One of the most consistent negative points of discussion is the inclusion of a USB Type-C 2.0 port. This is widely seen as an outdated standard, especially for a device in this segment, and users frequently lament the slow data transfer speeds. This particular hardware choice stands out as a clear area where the device falls short of modern expectations. Overall, the Nothing Phone (3) appeals strongly to users who value design innovation and a clean, fast software environment. Those who prioritize these aspects tend to overlook or accept the camera and USB port limitations. Conversely, users seeking a no-compromise camera experience or cutting-edge connectivity might find these drawbacks more significant, leading them to explore alternatives.

Frequently asked questions

How is the Nothing Phone (3) camera performance?

While the Nothing Phone (3)'s triple 50 MP camera system can capture detailed photos in good light, it may experience focus issues and inconsistencies in low-light conditions with the main and telephoto lenses. This causes it to lag behind flagship competitors.

Is the Nothing Phone (3) suitable for gaming?

Yes, the Nothing Phone (3) is well-suited for gaming. Powered by the Qualcomm Snapdragon 8s Gen 4 processor and 12 GB of RAM, it delivers a fluid performance for daily use and demanding games, with significant performance improvements over the Nothing Phone (2).

What is the Nothing Phone (3)'s battery life and charging speed?

The device features a 5150 mAh battery and 65W fast charging support, providing sufficient battery life for most users and quick recharges. Wireless charging is also available, though some users have noted that battery optimization could be better.

How long does Nothing Phone (3) receive software support?

Nothing promises five years of Android OS updates and seven years of security patches for the Nothing Phone (3), ensuring the device remains current and secure for an extended period. This commitment enhances the phone's longevity.

Is the Nothing Phone (3) water and dust resistant?

Yes, the Nothing Phone (3) is IP68 certified for water and dust resistance. This certification indicates a high level of protection against water and dust ingress, contributing to the device's overall durability.

What connectivity ports does the Nothing Phone (3) use?

The Nothing Phone (3) supports modern wireless standards like 5G, Wi-Fi 7, and Bluetooth 6.0. However, it uses a USB Type-C 2.0 port for physical connections, which might be a disadvantage for scenarios requiring high-speed data transfer.
