Back to School 2026: The 7 Best Student Laptops and Why Prices Exploded

Buying a laptop this year costs 15–30% more than last year — and it isn't a markup, it's a memory shortage. Which models still make sense, how to avoid the 8GB RAM trap, and should you buy now or wait? An honest guide with August 2026 pricing.

Back-to-school shopping is oddly difficult this year. The problem isn't a lack of options — it's that the laptop you bought for $700 last year now costs $850, and the retailer isn't offering you a single new feature for the difference.

The cause is simple and uncomfortable: memory. Tom's Hardware reported that conventional DRAM contract prices rose roughly 60% in a single quarter this spring, and IDC expects DRAM and NAND supply growth to stay below historical norms all year. The most visible consequence for students: Apple raised the MacBook Neo's price by $100 on June 25, 2026, from $599 to $699, and raised MacBook Air pricing at the same time. The net effect is a market where new laptops cost 15–30% more than they did last year.

This guide doesn't ignore that. Below are seven laptops, but more importantly, clear answers to three questions: what you can compromise on, what you can't, and whether buying this year is smart or foolish.

Learn the Rule First: In 2026, RAM Is Everything

You could evaluate fifteen different specs when buying a laptop. But this year there's one golden rule.

Four specs matter more than the rest, starting with memory: 16GB of RAM is the 2026 floor, and it is the single biggest factor in how many apps and tabs you can run at once. On Windows the rule is blunt: if you're choosing between more RAM or more SSD, always take the RAM — it's usually soldered, while storage you can expand externally.

Here's why it matters so much. The $500 HP OmniBook 3 offers a great OLED panel but only 8GB of RAM — and it's worse here because this is Windows on ARM, so anything not compiled natively runs through emulation, which eats memory. For a kid doing homework and YouTube, go for it. For an actual college student with 30 tabs, a PDF, Spotify, and Teams calls all at once, get 16GB.

Memory is the one component you usually can't add later on a thin laptop, so paying $75 now to double it is a reasonable hedge. There's a resale angle too: base-configuration machines with the minimum RAM depreciate faster than mid-tier configurations, because the used market prices them as already-obsolete.

The rule in short: Cut storage, cut screen quality, cut CPU if you must — don't cut RAM.

1. Apple MacBook Neo Ultrabook

This is Apple doing something it hadn't done in a decade. Apple spent ten years telling everyone the entry point to the Mac was near four figures. The MacBook Neo, released in March 2026, blows that doorway open as Apple's cheapest laptop even after the June 2026 price reset. It exists for one reason: to stop students, schools, and casual buyers from defaulting to a Chromebook or an iPad with a keyboard. The real story is the chip. Instead of an M-series processor it uses the A18 Pro from the iPhone 16 Pro (TSMC 3nm) — the first Mac to run an iPhone-class chip. And the performance is real: it outperforms the M1 MacBook Air on Geekbench 6 single-core (3,461 vs ~3,200) and handles 4K video editing adequately. Fanless passive cooling means completely silent operation. The limits are equally clear: an 8GB unified memory ceiling with no upgrade option, USB 2.0 on the right port, no Thunderbolt 4, no external display above 4K@60Hz, no keyboard backlight on the base model, and 20W charging only. There's an unexpected bonus: repairability. iFixit rated it Apple's most repairable laptop in 14 years — screwed battery, no parts pairing, modular ports. For a student keeping a machine four years, that's real value that never shows up on a spec sheet. This is the most-asked question in this guide, and the honest answer splits in two.

If you start school in September: buy. Waiting achieves nothing, because prices aren't dropping soon. IDC expects DRAM and NAND supply growth to stay below historical norms all year. There's no signal you'll pay less in October or November. Meanwhile back-to-school promotions are at their peak right now.

If your current laptop still works: wait. This may be the worst year in recent memory to upgrade. The same money bought a better machine last year, and you're getting no new feature for the difference.

One more tactic: whatever ends up in the backpack this fall, the machine it replaces is depreciating in a drawer right now — and in a market where new laptops cost 15–30% more than they did last year, that trade-in value covers a bigger share of the upgrade than it used to. Sell or trade in your old laptop; that money means more this year than usual.

Finally, always check education pricing. Education pricing still takes $100 off, which puts the 256GB MacBook Neo back at its original $599 for students, teachers, and parents buying for an enrolled student. Retailers may also still be selling remaining stock at launch pricing — Tom's Guide reported that Best Buy still had MacBook Neo units at $599 after Apple's increase. Compare Apple, authorised retailers and the education store before checkout.
